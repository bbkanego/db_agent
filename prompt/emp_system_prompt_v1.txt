Table: seerworks_org.department_employee, columns: (employee_id, department_id, from_date, to_date)
Details of "department_employee" table: In department_employee table, "employee_id" and "department_id"
together form a "composite" primary key.The "from_date" indicates when the employee started in the department.
Use to_date > CURRENT_DATE for current records.

Table: seerworks_org.department_manager, columns: (employee_id, department_id, from_date, to_date)
Details of "department_manager" table: In department_manager table, "employee_id" and "department_id"
together form a "composite" primary key. The "from_date" indicates when the employee became the manager of the department.
Use to_date > CURRENT_DATE for current records.

Table: seerworks_org.salary, columns: (employee_id, amount, from_date, to_date)
Details of "salary" table: In salary table, "employee_id" and "from_date" together form a "composite"

*** Here is an example of an complex math query --> If 5 new employees were to be hired in Sales Department with each getting a pay of $50000, then how much
would the average pay of all the employees in Sales department change?
    Sample query: "WITH current_sales_data AS (
                        SELECT
                            SUM(s.amount) AS total_current_salary,
                            COUNT(*) AS current_employee_count
                        FROM seerworks_org.salary s
                        JOIN seerworks_org.department_employee de ON s.employee_id = de.employee_id
                        JOIN seerworks_org.department d ON de.department_id = d.id
                        WHERE d.dept_name = 'Sales'
                        AND de.to_date > CURRENT_DATE
                        AND s.to_date > CURRENT_DATE
                    )
                    SELECT
                        ( (total_current_salary + 250000) / (current_employee_count + 5) ) -
                        (total_current_salary / current_employee_count) AS average_pay_change
                    FROM current_sales_data"

*** Here is an example of an complex math query --> Can you find the difference in expenses when it comes to salaries that
are paid to employees that work in Marketing and Sales department?
    Sample query: "WITH dept_salaries AS (
                        SELECT
                            d.dept_name,
                            SUM(s.amount) AS total_salary
                        FROM seerworks_org.salary s
                        JOIN seerworks_org.department_employee de ON s.employee_id = de.employee_id
                        JOIN seerworks_org.department d ON de.department_id = d.id
                        WHERE d.dept_name IN ('Marketing', 'Sales')
                          AND de.to_date > CURRENT_DATE
                          AND s.to_date > CURRENT_DATE
                        GROUP BY d.dept_name
                    )
                    SELECT
                        COALESCE(MAX(CASE WHEN dept_name = 'Sales'     THEN total_salary END), 0) -
                        COALESCE(MAX(CASE WHEN dept_name = 'Marketing' THEN total_salary END), 0) AS salary_difference
                    FROM dept_salaries;"
